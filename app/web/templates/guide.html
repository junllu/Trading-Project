The 3-minute morning If you only do one thing, do this.

Open the dashboard and read the top line of Flight Status. If it says GO, close the tab. That is the entire routine on a normal day, and most days are normal.

Everything below the last CAUTION row is deliberately dimmed. Those rows exist to be skipped — they are proof the system checked, not information you need to absorb.

Why it works this way A launch controller does not read their console; they scan a column of lights and look only at what is not green. A display that needs interpreting gets interpreted late. Everything nominal stays quiet on purpose.

What runs by itself Three things, none of which can place an order.

ComponentCadenceWhat it does
Live recorderevery 5 min, market hours Logs what the system would do, before outcomes are known. This is the only out-of-sample evidence the project can generate.
Orchestratorhourly Runs the research agents on their own cadences and writes their artefacts.
Panels4s – 10 min Quotes are fast; the status board and theme data are slow because they walk your trade history.

All of it starts with launch.bat. There is no second command.

What needs you One category, and it is structural — not an oversight.

Fundamentals, SEC filing facts and earnings dates arrive through the broker MCP. The portal holds no broker credentials by design, so Claude fetches those and commits them as dated snapshots. That dating is also what keeps them point-in-time honest — a snapshot you can see the age of cannot silently go stale.

The system tells you which pulls it needs rather than expecting you to know:

python -m app.agent.orchestrator

Look for the NEEDS A HUMAN block at the bottom.

Buttons that place orders

Read before pressing Run Strategy Tick, Start Strategy Loop and Run Daily Analysis place orders in the current mode. In paper that is harmless. Do not switch to live and press them: walk-forward validation put the conviction engine ahead of buy-and-hold in 1 of 6 out-of-sample folds. Automating it would only lose money more reliably.

Kill-Switch (top right) halts all execution immediately.

Flight Status Ordered by consequence. The top line always matters most.

StateMeansDo
ALARMcapital or evidence integrity affectedLook now.

A ! marks a capital-critical subsystem. NO DATA is reported rather than skipped — a check that quietly fails reads exactly like a check that passed, and that is how a real problem hides.

The two legs Separate books, separate edges, separate clocks.

Core (~80%)Options (~20%)
QuestionIs the thesis still true?Is there a mispriced event?
Horizonyears, to the 2028 peakweeks, 30–60 DTE
Cadencequarterly, when a filing landsdaily
Evidencefilings, peer multiples, growthvol rank, event calendar

They do not share a brain, and that is enforced in code. The same fact points opposite ways depending on horizon: MU's revenue growth being 97% price tells the core leg don't own this and tells the options leg premium is rich. One brain would have to pick, and be wrong half the time.

They meet at exactly one place — covered calls and cash-secured puts — because those are the only structures whose assignment hands the core pool shares it did not choose. Every other options structure risks only the sleeve's own premium and asks nobody.

Why the clocks differ Every public "AI hedge fund desk" writeup puts everything on one 6 AM cadence. On a book held to 2028 that is ~250 chances a year to talk yourself out of a thesis that has not changed. The core leg re-grades when new evidence exists — a filing — not every morning.

The decision queue One list instead of eleven reports.

Every record carries its own audit trail, so a decision read a week later can still be checked rather than merely remembered:

FieldWhy it is there
claimone sentence of what is true
evidencethe numbers, each with a source and an as_of, so a stale input is visible instead of inherited
falsifierwhat would prove this wrong. Required — a record without one is rejected
gateFINISH or PARK, decided by reversibility, never importance
ownerwhich agent produced it, so a wrong call is attributable

The agents Deterministic code, not language models.

Each owns exactly one result and has a cadence and a gate. Makers produce evidence and never judge it. Checkers judge and never produce. The chief routes and gates, and generates nothing.

That split is not tidiness. The thesis ledger's net-margin checkpoints all passed on ANET while the filing showed costs growing 46.9% against revenue's 37.7% — a checker reading its own preferred number. Only a second maker with a different input catches that.

In the Agents panel: a pulse means a tick fired, a sweep means an agent is running, a flash means a number actually changed. No motion is ambient.

What this system has actually proven The uncomfortable part, and the most valuable.

QuestionMeasured answer
Does the conviction algorithm beat buy-and-hold? No. 1 of 6 walk-forward folds. Deflated Sharpe below the bar that selection across 15 configs produces by chance.
Do the discretionary entries beat SPY? No. 40% hit rate at 63 days, 28% at a year. Median pick lost to the index; the positive average is the 2021 squeeze.
Does theme selection work? Yes. EV 4.1×, crypto 2.2×, ARM +$27,627 on a structural thesis.
What did exits cost? −$148,258 against $70,000 of realised profit across a decade.
The single most important number Exit timing cost more than twice what a decade of trading made. Three days — 2021-02-02, 2024-05-22, 2024-07-17 — each sold 17–30 names and bought nothing. They were portfolio reflexes, not stock decisions, and they caught the winners along with the losers. PLTR alone: −$94,006.

What to take from this. Your edge is picking themes with a structural backstop and holding them. It is not timing, and the record says so twice. The largest available gain is not a better entry — it is not repeating one mass liquidation.

Rules the system enforces

The approval line is drawn by reversibility

Research, screening, drafting and staging finish — none of it can be undone wrongly. Ordering, selling, deleting, overwriting and transferring park. Anything unclassified parks: an unknown action is treated as irreversible.

Iteration has a visible price

Every strategy configuration examined is recorded. Try 100 things and the best one's Sharpe is inflated by selection alone — that is exactly how a 16pp overfit gap appears. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio discounts for how many things you tried, so the bar rises every time you search harder.

Autonomy is earned and can be lost

A routine is promoted only on 5+ clean runs at a 100% pass rate, and is demoted automatically when its pass rate decays. Deliberately asymmetric: the cost of over-trusting a broken routine is money; the cost of under-trusting a good one is a manual run.

Silence is not neutrality

When a source has said nothing about a name, its slot is skipped — not scored 0.0. A missing opinion is not a neutral opinion.

Traps it watches for

A low multiple is not a buy signal

Memory trades at 23× because the market expects those earnings to fall — and the filings agree: 97% of MU's revenue growth is price, not volume. Cheap and correctly-cheap look identical on a valuation screen. That is why peak_margin_watch fires on record margin plus a low P/E in a cyclical sector.

A theme rising slower than the index is rotating out

Nothing looks wrong, you are still making money, and the opportunity cost is invisible. This is the case a price chart hides and a P&L never shows.

A sold position raises no alarm anywhere

It produces no drawdown, no row, no log entry. Its absence is silent by construction, which is why the re-entry table has to look for it deliberately — and why PLTR and RKLB went unnoticed for two years.

Gross margin moves before net margin

Buybacks, tax and one-time items sit between the business and the net line. ANET, ALAB and NOW all passed their net-margin checkpoints while their gross margins were already eroding.
