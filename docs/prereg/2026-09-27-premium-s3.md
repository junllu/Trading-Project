# Pre-registration — S3: premium selling after breakouts / breakdowns

Committed before the study is run.

## Framing (not arbitrage)

Covered calls and cash-secured puts SELL insurance. The edge, if any, is the
volatility risk premium: buyers overpay on average for moves that cost less
than the premium — paid for by rare large losses (a gap through the strike).
The question is therefore actuarial: after a given signal, what did the
insurance actually cost, compared with what the market charges for it?

No free historical option prices exist here, so a conventional options
backtest would have to assume premiums, and the assumption would decide the
result. Instead:

1. HISTORY (this study) measures the realised payout of the option that would
   have been sold, and restates it as a **fair vol**: the Black-Scholes vol at
   which the premium equals that average payout. Scale-free as
   **fair ratio = fair vol / trailing 20-session realised vol** at the signal.
2. LIVE (separate step) compares market implied vol to the fair vol for
   current signals. Selling is favoured only where IV > fair vol.

## Structures (fixed)

Sold at the signal bar's CLOSE, expiry 20 sessions later (≈ 28 calendar
days), strike at Black-Scholes |delta| = 0.25 using the trailing 20-session
realised vol (known at the time) as the vol input, rate 4.5%.

- **Short put** (the cash-secured put): payout = max(K − S_T, 0)
- **Short call** (the covered call): payout = max(S_T − K, 0)

## Metrics per trial

Events; empirical assignment rate vs the 0.25 model delta; mean payout (% of
spot); fair vol and fair ratio; 5% CVaR of the short-option P&L (% of spot);
and the difference in fair ratio vs control, monthly blocks.

## Pass rule

Fair-ratio difference vs control < 0 (cheaper insurance) with one-sided
**t ≤ −2.24** (5%, Bonferroni for 4), and the sign the same in both eras
(≤ 2022, ≥ 2023). A pass says "this signal lowers the historical cost of the
insurance"; whether the market's price is high enough to sell is the separate
live comparison.
